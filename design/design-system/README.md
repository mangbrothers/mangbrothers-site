Mangiapane Brothers Lawn & Snow LLC is a family-owned property maintenance company in Livonia and Westland, Michigan. The brand should feel **premium and reliable**: established, trustworthy and personal, never corporate or flashy.

## Brand foundation

- **Mission:** To provide professional property maintenance with exceptional customer service.
- **Tagline:** Reliable Service. Uncompromising Standards.
- **Positioning:** Full-service property maintenance for those who take pride in their home.
- **Brand goal (what people say behind our backs):** "I trust Russel."

## Brand identity

**In one line:** the Mangiapane name as a quiet mark of quality, delivered plainly and reliably.

- **Audience:** older homeowners who want their property cared for properly and don't want to think about it.
- **Why they choose us:** reliability and quality of work.
- **Personality:** dependable and confident.
- **Feel:** premium first, with neighborly warmth underneath. The takeaway from any piece: this feels high-end.
- **Style:** classic with a modern edge. Pristine, traditional and quietly confident; clean and current in execution.
- **The name:** "Mangiapane" carries real weight and is treated almost as a mark of quality. "Family-owned" is secondary.
- **Voice:** plainspoken and direct. No fluff, no hype.
- **Kept internal:** American and Christian values guide how the business runs but do not appear in marketing.
- **Avoid:** the typical landscaper look (lime green, cartoon grass, clip-art mowers) and anything that feels like a corporate franchise.
- **Growth:** regional expansion with a personal feel. The brand must look polished and repeatable without going generic.
- **Internal reference point:** private golf and country club grounds: pristine, traditional, understated. For direction only; never use this comparison in copy or branding.

## Logo

The logo is a traced oak tree standing on a rule above "Mangiapane Brothers" (Newsreader Display SemiBold) and "LAWN & SNOW LLC" (Libre Franklin SemiBold capitals) set between two rules. Every file is vector (SVG and PDF) with a transparent background, so it stays sharp from a favicon to a truck wrap and can print as spot colors. Use the supplied files only: never retype the name, redraw the tree, recolor, stretch, rotate, add effects or rearrange the parts.

**Versions**

| Version | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| **Stacked** (primary) | The default. Print pieces, signs, flyers, door hangers, letterhead, anywhere with room to stack. |
| **Horizontal** | Wide, short spaces: website header, email signature, business cards, truck doors. |
| **Tree icon** | When the name already appears nearby or space is tiny: social profile pictures, favicons, hats, stickers, embroidery accents. |
| **Avatar** | A ready-made square of the tree icon on `cream` for social profiles; 64px favicon included. |

**Color versions** (each layout comes in all four)

| Color version | Background |
| --- | --- |
| Full color: tree in `oak` and `leaf`, name and rules `oak`, "LAWN & SNOW LLC" `charcoal` | `cream`, `paper` or white. The default. |
| One color · oak | Light backgrounds when only one ink is available: embroidery, stamps, vinyl, invoices |
| One color · black | Black-and-white printing, faxes, newspaper ads |
| One color · cream | `oak`, `charcoal` and other dark backgrounds, including shirts and dark photos |

**Rules**
- Never put the full-color logo on `oak`, `charcoal` or a busy photo; use the one-color cream version, or place it on a `cream` panel.
- Clear space on every side equals the height of the capital M in "Mangiapane." Keep text, edges and other graphics out of it.
- Minimum sizes: stacked 1.25 in (160px) wide, horizontal 1.5 in (200px) wide, tree icon 0.3 in (24px) tall. Below those, switch to the tree icon.
- Use SVG for the web and PDF for print vendors. PNG is for tools that won't take vector files, such as some Canva and social uploads.
- One logo per piece. Don't pair the stacked logo and the tree icon in the same layout.

**Retired:** the earlier raster logo (Baskerville lettering, thick and thin weights). Printed materials using it stay in circulation until they run out; new pieces use this kit only.

Also retired: the MB monogram and the "Mangiapane Bros." lockup. The tree icon replaces both.

## Color

The palette leans dark for a premium, professional feel, but layouts stay open and light. The darkness comes from deep, muted colors, not dark backgrounds.

| Token | Hex | Role | Share of a layout |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `cream` | #EADDC5 | Background canvas | 45–50% |
| `paper` | #F4EDE0 | Cards and panels on cream | 5–10% |
| `oak` | #023A1D | Primary | 25–30% |
| `charcoal` | #1A1C18 | Text, dark bands | about 10% |
| `brown` | #4E220C | Headlines, links | about 5% |
| `leaf` | #166B3B | Small signals | 3% or less |
| `gold` | #C9A15A | Accent on dark | 3% or less |

**How the shares keep it coherent**
- `cream` and `paper` together make up about 55–60% of a layout. `paper` is always one step lighter than the `cream` behind it, so boxes such as cards read as sitting on top; it is never used alone or as a section background.
- Light colors carry the space so the piece feels open; `oak` carries the weight so it reads premium. If a piece starts to feel heavy, it has too much `oak` or `charcoal`.
- `brown`, `leaf` and `gold` are seasoning. Each appears in small, repeated touches, never as a big block. When any of them grows into a large area, the palette starts to look muddy.
- Every piece should have one clear dark anchor (usually an `oak` section) and one accent moment (usually a `gold` button on that section).

**Where each color goes**
- `oak`: hero and header sections, primary buttons on light grounds, photo frames.
- `charcoal`: body text, plus thin bands such as trust strips, footers and sticky call bars.
- `brown`: section headlines, text links, dividers and testimonials, always as type or line on `cream` or `paper`.
- `leaf`: checkmarks, status badges, "most booked" style tags.
- `gold`: call buttons, short rules and eyebrow labels on `oak` or `charcoal`.
- `paper`: cards and panels sitting on `cream`.

**Pairings that work**
- `charcoal` text on `cream` or `paper` for body copy.
- `brown` headlines on `cream` or `paper`.
- `cream` text on `oak`, `charcoal` or `leaf`.
- `gold` buttons with `charcoal` text on `oak` or `charcoal`.

**Never**
- `brown` as a large fill, or `brown` next to `charcoal` or `oak`. They are too close in darkness.
- `gold` on `cream` or `paper`. It nearly vanishes; use `brown` or `oak` accents there instead.
- `leaf` as a large field or as small text.
- `charcoal` as the dominant background of a piece.
- Gradients, metallic effects or tints outside these seven colors.

## Typography

Two typefaces. `display` (Newsreader) gives the brand its voice; `sans` (Libre Franklin) does everything functional. Both are free Google Fonts, licensed for web, print, signage and apparel.

| Family | Role | Weights |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Newsreader, **display optical size** | Headlines, prices, pull quotes | Light 300 on the web; Medium 500 in print |
| Libre Franklin | Body, labels, buttons, phone numbers, forms, fine print | Regular 400, SemiBold 600, Bold 700 |

**Rules**
- Newsreader is for headlines only. Never set paragraphs, buttons, forms or fine print in it; long passages read like a newspaper.
- Always use Newsreader's display (large) optical size for headlines. It is the finer, sharper cut that gives the brand its elegance. On the web, use the `headline-*` styles or `font-optical-sizing: auto`; in tools that offer only one Newsreader, upload the display-size font file (see Tools).
- Headlines in `brown` on `cream` or `paper`, and in `cream` on `oak`. Prices in `oak` on light grounds, `gold` or `cream` on dark.
- Keep spacing tight. Headlines at -0.02em; eyebrow capitals at no more than 0.03em. Wide letter-spacing is off-brand.
- Capitals only for short eyebrow labels, never for headlines or sentences.
- One style per headline. Never italicize or bold a single word inside a headline, and never mix roman and italic in one.
- Light weight (300) for web headlines and prices: thin, large and quiet is the luxury look. Never use Light below 26px or in print, where it breaks up; print headlines stay Medium 500. Never heavy bold.
- Body copy, buttons and labels stay Libre Franklin Regular or SemiBold. Thin small text is hard for older readers.
- Body text is never smaller than 16px on the web or 9pt in print. The audience skews older.
- Anything read from a distance (yard signs, truck graphics, door-hanger phone numbers): Libre Franklin Bold. Serif detail is lost at a distance.

**Type scale (web):** `headline-hero` 76px (42px on phones), `headline-xl` 56px, `headline-lg` 44px, `headline-md` 30px, `price` 28px, `quote` 36px italic, `eyebrow` 13px caps, `card-title` 20px, `body-lg` 19px, `body` 17px, `body-sm` 14.5px, `button` 17px, `caption` 13px. Scale proportionally for print.

**Tools**
- **Canva:** both fonts are in Canva's library, but Canva's Newsreader is the standard cut, not the display one. With Canva Pro, download Newsreader from Google Fonts and upload the display-size files to the Brand Kit for headlines.
- **Google Docs:** both are under Font → More fonts. Docs offers only the standard Newsreader cut, which is fine for letters and invoices.
- **Word and desktop apps:** install the font files.
